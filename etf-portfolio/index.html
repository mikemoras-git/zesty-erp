ETF Portfolio

Your monthly routine (about 10 minutes)

  1. Transfer your monthly amount (default €1,000; the first time, plus your €2,500 starting amount) to Interactive Brokers (IBKR) in EUR. Record it here as a Deposit, or skip this if you don't want to track cash.
  2. Open Buy plan. It reads your live prices, current holdings and targets, and lists exactly which ETFs to buy and how many shares.
  3. In IBKR, search each ETF by ISIN, choose the exchange shown (Xetra = IBIS/IBIS2, Euronext Amsterdam = AEB), currency EUR, and place a limit order during market hours (Xetra and Euronext: 10:00–18:30 Athens time).
  4. Click Record next to each line and type the real fill price and commission from IBKR.
  5. Bought only part of the plan? No problem. The next plan starts again from what you actually own, so it adds whatever is still missing.
  6. Market drop? When an ETF falls below your average price, the plan suggests extra dip money (default €500 at −10%, €1,000 at −20%) and invests it only in the ETFs that fell, which lowers your average price. You also get a notification while the app is open.

Prices and dividends refresh automatically while the app is open. Distributing ETFs' dividends are detected and recorded as pending. Confirm them against your IBKR statement.

About the 7% target

No ETF portfolio can guarantee 7% a year. Global stocks have returned roughly 7–9% a year on average over long periods, but with years of −20% to −50%. Over 2000–2009 world stocks returned about zero in euros. Treat 7% as a long-term average you aim for (10+ years), not a yearly minimum.

  • The default model is 100% stocks: 70% growth (global, Nasdaq-100, small caps) and 30% dividend ETFs. Its expected return is about 7.1% under the assumptions in ETFs & targets, which you can edit.
  • A 30% dividend sleeve does not mean 30% of your return comes as cash. Those ETFs yield about 3%, so the whole portfolio pays about 0.9% a year in dividends.
  • In Greece, accumulating ETFs are slightly more tax-efficient than distributing ones (see Taxes below). Lowering the dividend share in Settings raises expected after-tax return a little.
  • Keep an emergency fund (3–6 months of expenses) outside this portfolio, so you never have to sell during a crash.

The buy-low / sell-high strategy: what the research says

1. Waiting in cash for dips loses money on average

Maggiulli's study "Even God Couldn't Beat Dollar-Cost Averaging" found that a perfect dip-buyer, who held cash and bought only at exact market bottoms, lost to plain weekly/monthly buying in about 70% of 40-year periods. Markets rise more often than they fall, so cash waiting for a dip misses the gains. Vanguard also found that investing a lump sum immediately beats spreading it out about ⅔ of the time.

→ The app never tells you to hold money back. Every month's €1,000 is invested. Extra dip money is on top, from savings you have anyway. That's how buying dips adds value without the cost of waiting in cash.

2. Tilting new money toward what fell works and costs nothing

When an ETF is X% below your average price, or well below its 52-week high, the buy plan gives it a bigger share of that month's money (×1.25 / ×1.5 / ×2 at three levels you set). You buy more cheap units without timing the market.

3. Sell on allocation drift, not just on price gains

Selling only because a price is +X% above your average price has no research support: winners often keep winning (momentum). What works is threshold rebalancing:

  • 5/25 rule (Larry Swedroe): rebalance an asset when it drifts 5 percentage points from target, or 25% of its target weight, whichever comes first.
  • Opportunistic rebalancing (Daryanani, Journal of Financial Planning, 2008): check often (every 1–2 weeks) but act only when an asset breaches a ~20–25% relative band. In backtests this beat calendar rebalancing, because it trades only when moves are large.

→ The app's "Trim" signal fires only when an ETF is up by your take-profit % and overweight beyond the band. Most rebalancing happens for free by steering monthly buys to underweight ETFs.

Presets (Settings → Strategy)

  • Opportunistic rebalancing + dip tilt (recommended): buy-more alerts at −5 / −10 / −20% vs average price, trim at +25% only if overweight, bands 5pp / 25%.
  • Plain DCA: no signals, just monthly allocation to targets.
  • Aggressive dip buyer: earlier and stronger tilts (−3 / −7 / −15%), trims at +20% even if not overweight. More trades and more fees, with no proven extra return.

Taxes in Greece (check with your accountant)

Summary as understood in Nov 2026. Rules change, and sources do not fully agree on how distributions from foreign ETFs are classified. Have a Greek accountant (λογιστής) confirm before filing.
  • Capital gains on EU/EEA UCITS funds, which includes Irish-domiciled ETFs like all those in the model, are listed as tax-exempt for individuals (PwC Greece tax summary). Some guides still say sales should be reported on the E1 form. The Tax report exports your sales with gross proceeds and gains for this.
  • Dividends / distributions are declared on the E1 and generally taxed at the Greek dividend rate, 5% (a setting in the app). Irish ETFs normally pay distributions gross (no Irish withholding), so the 5% is paid through your annual return. Any foreign tax withheld is credited.
  • Accumulating ETFs reinvest inside the fund, so there is no yearly dividend to declare. That is why they are the most tax-efficient choice for a Greek resident.
  • Interest IBKR pays on cash is taxed at 15%.
  • Source of funds: money you invest must be covered by declared income or savings (πόθεν έσχες). IBKR reports your account to AADE automatically under CRS.
  • Use EUR at the ECB rate on the transaction date for non-EUR amounts. The app stores the rate on every transaction and can fetch the ECB rate for you.

Currency: invest in euros (recommended)

  • Buy the EUR listings (Xetra/IBIS, Euronext/AEB, Borsa Italiana). You avoid a conversion on every trade, and your cost basis and taxes are naturally in EUR. All default ETFs are EUR lines.
  • Trading currency ≠ currency risk. A world ETF bought in EUR still owns ~65% US companies, so its value moves with EUR/USD. That exposure is normal and diversifying over the long term. Hedged ETFs cost more and have historically not paid off for stocks.
  • The Euro Dividend Aristocrats ETF in the dividend sleeve holds euro-area companies, which reduces your FX exposure where it matters most for income.
  • NOK / USD: record them as-is. The app converts at the stored rate, and the holdings table splits your P/L into price effect and FX effect. Convert currency inside IBKR (very cheap) rather than at a bank.

Finding the ETFs at Interactive Brokers

  • In TWS / IBKR Mobile / Client Portal, type the ISIN (e.g. IE00BK5BQT80). It's the most reliable search. Choose the listing with the exchange and currency shown in ETFs & targets.
  • EU retail investors can only buy UCITS ETFs (they have a KID document), so US-listed ETFs like VOO or SCHD are blocked. Every ETF in the model is UCITS and Irish-domiciled.
  • Fees: IBKR Tiered ≈ 0.05% (min ~€1.25 + small exchange fees). Fixed = 0.05%, min €3. With €1,000 a month, each order costs about 0.1–0.5%. The plan avoids orders below your minimum order setting (default €250), so it places 2–4 orders a month instead of six small ones.
  • IBKR does not offer fractional shares on most EU-listed ETFs, so the plan uses whole shares. Leftover cash rolls over to the next plan.
  • Check each ETF's current factsheet and fees on justETF before buying. Ticker or fee details can change.
